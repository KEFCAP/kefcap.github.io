HOW IT WORKS — The strategy holds a concentrated basket of large, established dividend-paying companies selected by a proprietary quality screener developed and owned by KEF CAPITAL LLC. The basket is reconstituted and rebalanced once a year and typically holds 23 to 29 names. Position sizes come out of the screen itself in 2% increments — a company that clears more of the screen's quality tests carries more weight — and every year's weights sum to exactly 100%, which doubles as the run's validity check. Each year's basket is built only from holdings data that was already public before that year began. Two switches drive this page: the start year decides when the money goes to work, and the mode decides what happens to the dividends — reinvested, or taken out as income. Every figure here is simulated: no capital was invested, and no costs, spreads, or taxes are deducted. Read the methodology and disclosure panels at the bottom before drawing conclusions from any of it.

METHODOLOGY AND KNOWN LIMITATIONS — Each year's basket is reconstructed from regulatory filings whose reporting date falls on or before December 31 of the prior year, so every holding shown for a given year was already a matter of public record before that year began. An earlier version of this study used filings dated one to two months into the year being measured; correcting that cost more than two points of annual return, and the figures on this page are the corrected ones. What remains: the rule itself was written with hindsight. Point-in-time data fixes the inputs, not the fact that the screen was designed in 2026 and then tested backward — that limitation cannot be engineered away and is the reason a six-year result is a starting point rather than a conclusion. Beyond it: the study begins in 2020 because the underlying filing regime does not reach further back; drawdown is measured calendar-year rather than peak-to-trough, so real intra-year declines were deeper than the table shows; turnover is high and no trading costs, spreads, slippage, or taxes are deducted anywhere; and the income figures are the basket's own trailing cash yields inside a backtest, not a forward yield and not a distribution anyone is promising.
IMPORTANT DISCLOSURES. The performance shown on this page is hypothetical and backtested. It does not represent the results of actual trading and no client or proprietary capital was invested in this strategy during the periods shown. Hypothetical results have inherent limitations: they are prepared with the benefit of hindsight in the design of the rules, they do not reflect the impact that material economic or market factors might have had on decision-making in real time, and they cannot account for the effect of financial risk on actual trading. Results assume annual rebalancing with no transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, slippage, management fees, or taxes. Dividend and income figures reflect cash dividends actually paid by the underlying companies in the years shown, applied to a portfolio nobody held; they are not a forecast and not a distribution commitment. Index and ETF comparisons are shown for reference only; an investor cannot invest directly in an index, and the benchmarks differ from this strategy in holdings, concentration, and volatility.
